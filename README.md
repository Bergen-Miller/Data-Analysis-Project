The question we attempt to answer is the following. How do tax shocks at the state or county level affect immigration, and does this effect depend on the incomes of the people moving? Alternatively, do people 'vote with their feet' when their state or city raises taxes by moving to lower-tax counties, and is this effect predominantly observed in high-income tax-filers?

Our data all come from the IRS Statistics of Income county-level incomes and tax data and immigration data. Our time period is from 2011-2022.

We construct a dataset consisting of pairs of counties across years, with information on the two counties' effective tax rates, changes in tax rates from the previous year, other income data, and flows of people between the two counties (in each direction).

There is variation in effective tax rates in every county in every year, but to find exogenous tax shocks we use the following policy changes:

1. New Jersey changed their top marginal income tax from 8.97% on income exceeding $500k to 10.75% on income exceeding $5M in 2018. Then in 2020 (effective starting 2021), they lowered the $5M threshold to $1M.

2.  New York State changed the top marginal income tax from 6.85% to 9.85% on income exceeding $1M effective in 2021.

3.  Delaware repealed their estate tax, which was previously 16% on inheritences beyond $5.49M, effective 2018.
